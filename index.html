About

About Me

I'm a first year PhD student in the Skaggs Graduate School of Chemical and Biological Sciences at Scripps Research. Before this, I earned my B.S. in Neurobiology at UC San Diego and spent over three years as a research technician at the La Jolla Institute for Immunology, studying immune and metabolic dysregulation in disease. I now aim to bridge immunology and metabolism to uncover the blueprints of neurological disease pathology, in search of new drug targets for both rare and common conditions.

Outside the lab, I'm deeply committed to mentoring first-generation and community college pathway students into research, in service of a larger vision: making science accessible to everyone, no matter where they start. Beyond that, you'll usually find me on a hiking trail or around a board game table.

Research

Research interests

A few threads that run through my current and recent work.

Neuroimmune Remodeling in Disease

My work on a pre-plaque Alzheimer's mouse model showed me that microglia begin remodeling long before disease is detectable. Writing a review on sex-biased immunometabolism deepened that insight further, revealing how metabolic programs like glycolytic reprogramming and purine signaling actively shape these divergent trajectories across AD, MS, PD, and ALS. Together, these experiences convinced me that understanding when and why neuroimmune systems diverge is essential to catching disease earlier. This a question I will continue to pursue.

Immunometabolism

Metabolism sets the terms inflammation plays by. At LJI, I studied how purine metabolism drives mitochondrial depletion syndrome, fueling innate immune activation and lipid accumulation, and studied how a single bioactive lipid can disrupt a neutrophil's homeostasis to redirect its fate. I'm carrying these questions forward into aging and neurodegeneration.

Therapeutics & Drug Discovery

I've tested therapeutic strategies ranging from JAK inhibition for DGUOK deficiency to metabolite supplementation for checkpoint blockade side effects. Now, with over 75% of people needing neurological treatment in low-income countries unable to access care (WHO, 2024), I'm driven to help close that gap, turning mechanism into medicine that reaches the people who need it.
