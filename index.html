About

About Me

I'm a first year PhD student in the Skaggs Graduate School of Chemical and Biological Sciences at Scripps Research. Before this, I earned my B.S. in Neurobiology at UC San Diego and spent over three years as a research technician at the La Jolla Institute for Immunology, studying immune and metabolic dysregulation in disease. I now aim to bridge immunology and metabolism to uncover the blueprints of neurological disease pathology, in search of new drug targets for both rare and common conditions.

Outside the lab, I'm deeply committed to mentoring first-generation and community college pathway students into research, in service of a larger vision: making science accessible to everyone, no matter where they start. Beyond that, you'll usually find me on a hiking trail or around a board game table.

Research

Research interests

A few threads that run through my current and recent work.

Neuroimmune Remodeling in Disease

In a pre-plaque Alzheimer's model, I watched microglia begin remodeling long before disease ever declared itself, a quiet divergence that writing a review on sex-biased immunometabolism helped me trace back to programs like glycolytic reprogramming and purine signaling. If we can learn to read these early signals, we might catch disease before it takes hold.

Immunometabolism

Metabolism sets the terms inflammation plays by. At LJI, I studied how purine metabolism drives mitochondrial depletion syndrome, fueling innate immune activation and lipid accumulation, and studied how a single bioactive lipid can disrupt a neutrophil's homeostasis to redirect its fate. I'm carrying these questions forward into aging and neurodegeneration.

Advanced Imaging for Biological Discovery

Biology happens in space, not just in a Western blot lane or a sequencing readout — and imaging is often the only way to prove it. From nanogold TEM labeling to quantitative confocal morphometry and expansion microscopy, I've built a toolkit for seeing biology where it actually happens. The where is where the mechanism lives.

Therapeutics & Drug Discovery

I've tested therapeutic strategies ranging from JAK inhibition for DGUOK deficiency to metabolite supplementation for checkpoint blockade side effects. Now, with over 75% of people needing neurological treatment in low-income countries unable to access care (WHO, 2024), I'm driven to help close that gap, turning mechanism into medicine that reaches the people who need it.

MARCH 2025 · ORAL PRESENTATION

Deoxyguanosine Kinase Regulates Innate Immune Response and Lipid Accumulation in HepG2 Cells

La Jolla Institute for Immunology Retreat, Lake Arrowhead, CA

My very first oral presentation. My PI, Dr. Sonia Sharma, trusted me — a tech at the time — to speak at an institute-wide retreat in front of some of the field's most accomplished immunologists. The night before, I told my coworkers about a nightmare: one particular PI grilling me relentlessly with questions. The next day, it actually happened — except it was one question, and it wasn't so bad. I learned to push back against the nerves, because it's never quite as bad as you imagine.

My path

Life as a transfer student

I transferred to UC San Diego from Diablo Valley College, without much sense of what a research career would actually require. The college's Forensics Speech and Debate team ended up being one of the more formative parts of that time — I placed third at the Northern California Championships, and more than the result itself, it taught me how to slow down and actually communicate with a room instead of just getting through material.

Transferring came with a wake-up call, too. Not long after I arrived, I was told that if I wasn't already working in a lab, I was already behind for PhD applications. I hadn't known research experience mattered this early, and it worried me. But I also knew I only had so much time left at school, so I found a way in.

That's part of why mentoring community college and transfer students means so much to me now — I want to be someone who tells them earlier what caught me off guard, so they have more room to plan than I did.

I'm on a campus that Herbert Wertheim, also a transfer student, gave generously to. I don't know that the two are connected, but it's hard not to wonder whether people who've had to fight their way in are the ones who remember it.
